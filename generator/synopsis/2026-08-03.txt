Two stories set the tone, and they pushed the same direction: risk on. Washington and Tokyo confirmed a coordinated intervention to prop up the yen — the first joint move in years — and Brent fell more than five percent after the White House called off planned strikes on Iran and talks were set to resume Monday. Cheaper oil and a calmer Middle East did what they usually do: the S&P 500 rose about one percent, the VIX collapsed twelve percent to the high 15s, and Treasury yields eased across the curve.

The wire told the yen story in three stages worth noticing. Asian desks reported the intervention as fact before the US open; by mid-morning the American desks had moved to whether it can stick, with strategists split on whether a one-day, multi-billion-dollar push means anything against a structural rate gap. That gap between what happened and what it means is where tomorrow's trade lives.

Elsewhere, AstraZeneca confirmed it has explored a roughly $400 billion tie-up with Bristol Myers Squibb — analysts used the word "puzzled" more than "excited" — and OPEC+ agreed its September output hike, completing the rollback of voluntary cuts into a falling price. Watch Thursday's August CPI ahead of the September FOMC, and watch whether the yen holds 157: a failed intervention repriced faster than a successful one ever does.
