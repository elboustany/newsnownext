Copy this file to YYYY-MM-DD.txt and write over it. 200–300 words. Until that
file exists, the day's recap page is generated but carries noindex and stays out
of the sitemap — that guard is deliberate, see README.

Blank line between paragraphs. Plain text, no HTML, no markdown.

Structure that works:

Paragraph one — what actually moved and by how much. A number in the first
sentence. "Brent closed 5% lower at $61.40" beats "oil markets were volatile".

Paragraph two — why, and what the wire got wrong. This is the paragraph that
makes the page worth indexing. If every source ran the same explanation, say
which one broke it first and which ones followed. If the explanation changed
between the London and New York opens, say that — it's the thing our timeline
shows and nobody else's recap does.

Paragraph three — what to watch tomorrow. One or two specifics: a data release,
an earnings print, a central bank speaker. Not "markets will remain volatile".

Rules:

- Never paste a headline verbatim. Summarise across sources in your own words.
- No article text from any publisher, in any length. Headlines link out; that is
  the whole legal position and it does not survive being casually broken.
- If you used an LLM for a first pass, edit it. Unedited AI market summaries are
  exactly what search spam policy targets, and this site cannot afford to be
  classified that way.
- Under 200 words of real substance, don't publish it. A noindex recap costs
  nothing. A thin indexed one costs the domain.
