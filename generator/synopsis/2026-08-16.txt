A weekend tape, but not a quiet one. The UAE said an ADNOC vessel was attacked near the Strait of Hormuz and pointed at Iran, urging the waterway's reopening, and crude firmed toward the low 80s on the news. That headline lands on a market that had spent the week relaxing: the VIX sits in the mid 14s and gold holds above 4,400, which is the odd couple worth watching. Volatility priced for calm while the oldest hedge in the book sits at records is a disagreement, and one of them is wrong.

The rest of the weekend wire is geopolitics with market edges. Colombia's new president asked Washington to suspend tariffs after a deadly earthquake, a Spanish fighter downed a drone that crossed into Romanian airspace, and the China-Europe Arctic shipping route moved to regular weekly service, a small line with long consequences for freight and for who controls the shortcuts.

Nothing on the calendar until the week turns, and then it builds: Jackson Hole opens August 27, where the Fed chair's keynote has a habit of resetting rate expectations for the autumn. Between now and then, watch whether Hormuz stays a headline or becomes a premium in the barrel again. Tanker insurance reprices faster than crude does.
